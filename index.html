Death by Drag: Solar Storms and Satellites

Abstract

Solar storms can have a serious effect on satellites orbiting Earth. When the Sun releases large amounts of energy, it can heat the upper atmosphere. This causes the atmosphere to expand, meaning that satellites in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) experience more air resistance, also known as atmospheric drag.

This article explains how solar storms affect satellites, why they can cause satellites to lose altitude and what engineers can do to reduce the risk of satellites falling out of orbit.

1. Introduction

Thousands of satellites orbit Earth. They are used for GPS, communication, weather forecasting and internet services. Many of these satellites operate in Low Earth Orbit, which is usually between around 160 km and 2,000 km above Earth's surface.

Although space is often thought of as completely empty, there are still small amounts of gas in the upper atmosphere. These particles can slow satellites down over time.

This becomes a bigger problem during solar storms because the atmosphere can become much thicker. This creates more drag and can cause satellites to lose altitude faster than expected.

2. How Solar Storms Work

The Sun constantly releases energy into space. Sometimes it produces particularly powerful events called solar flares and coronal mass ejections.

A solar flare is a sudden release of energy and radiation from the Sun. A coronal mass ejection (CME) is a large amount of charged particles travelling away from the Sun.

When these particles reach Earth, they can disturb Earth's magnetic field. This is known as a geomagnetic storm.

The energy from these events heats the upper atmosphere. As the atmosphere heats up, it expands further into space. This means that satellites can experience more atmospheric drag.

Simple explanation

Solar storm → Atmosphere heats up → Atmosphere expands → More drag on satellites → Satellites lose altitude.

3. What is Atmospheric Drag?

Atmospheric drag is a force that slows down an object as it moves through a gas or fluid. On Earth, we experience this when moving through air.

Satellites experience a similar force because there are still small amounts of gas in Low Earth Orbit.

When a satellite experiences more drag, it loses energy. This causes its orbit to become lower. If the satellite loses too much altitude, it may eventually enter the atmosphere and burn up.

What affects atmospheric drag?

  • The density of the atmosphere.
  • The speed of the satellite.
  • The size and shape of the satellite.
  • The amount of solar activity.

A simplified equation for atmospheric drag is:

Drag = 1/2 × Density × Speed² × Area × Drag Coefficient

This equation shows that drag increases when atmospheric density or the satellite's exposed area increases.

4. The 2022 Starlink Incident

In February 2022, SpaceX launched 49 Starlink satellites. However, shortly after launch, a geomagnetic storm caused the upper atmosphere to expand.

This increased atmospheric drag on the satellites. Many of them were still in a relatively low orbit, meaning they were particularly affected by the increased drag.

Around 38 of the 49 satellites were eventually lost and re-entered Earth's atmosphere.

This incident showed how solar activity can have a major effect on satellites, even when they are working correctly. It also showed why space weather forecasting is important.

Why did this happen?

The main problem was increased atmospheric density. The satellites experienced more drag than expected and could not raise their orbits enough to survive.

5. How Can We Protect Satellites?

1. Better space weather forecasting

Scientists can monitor the Sun and look for solar activity that could affect Earth. If a storm is expected, satellite operators can prepare for increased atmospheric drag.

2. Changing satellite position

Satellites can sometimes change their position to reduce the amount of surface area facing the direction of travel. This can reduce atmospheric drag.

3. Keeping satellites at a higher altitude

Satellites at higher altitudes generally experience less atmospheric drag because the atmosphere is thinner. This can help them remain in orbit for longer.

4. Better satellite design

Engineers can design satellites to reduce drag and improve their ability to change orbit. This can make them more resistant to changes in atmospheric conditions.

6. Conclusion

Solar storms can cause serious problems for satellites in Low Earth Orbit. They heat the upper atmosphere, causing it to expand and become denser. This increases atmospheric drag and can cause satellites to lose altitude.

The 2022 Starlink incident showed how quickly these conditions can affect satellites. However, better forecasting, improved satellite designs and changes to satellite positioning can help reduce the risks.

As more satellites are launched into orbit, understanding space weather will become increasingly important.
